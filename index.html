    Which bridges

    Google Earth file

    One pin per bridge, colored by condition and grouped into Poor, Fair and Good folders. Click a pin in Google Earth to see the bridge's ratings, facts and links back to this app and to Street View.

    How to open it

    • Google Earth on the web: open earth.google.com, select Projects, then New project, then Import KML file from computer (it accepts .kmz files too).
    • Google Earth Pro (desktop): select File, then Open, and choose the file.

    Excel spreadsheet

    One row per bridge with every field in this app: condition and the rating of each part, open, posted or closed, flood erosion risk, traffic, size, type, last inspection, live camera, map location and a link to the bridge in this app. A second sheet explains the source and the ratings.

    Good to know

    • The first row stays in place as you scroll, and every column has a filter button.
    • Ratings are numbers from 0 (failed) to 9 (excellent). A blank rating means that part is not rated separately for that bridge.

    About this data

    Where it comes from

    • Bridges: , published by the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA). It holds every public bridge over 20 feet in West Virginia. Ratings come from WVDOH's routine inspections; most inspection dates in this file are from 2023 and 2024.
    • Live cameras: WV511 traffic cameras, run by WVDOH. Camera list checked .
    • Map: USGS The National Map imagery and topographic maps (public domain). 3D hills: AWS Terrain Tiles (open data).
    • Street View and Google Earth links open Google's own sites.

    How to read the condition

    • Inspectors rate each main part from 9 (excellent) to 0 (failed): the deck, the beams that hold it up, and the supports it sits on. Culverts get one rating.
    • A part rated 7 to 9 is Good, 5 to 6 is Fair, 0 to 4 is Poor. A bridge's overall condition is its weakest part.
    • Poor does not mean unsafe. Bridges that are not safe for some loads are posted with weight limits or closed; the bridge panel shows which.
    • Flood erosion risk means the bridge is listed as scour critical: its supports could be undermined if a flood washes away the streambed around them.

    Limits

    • The data is as of the federal file, not today. Bridges may have been repaired, inspected again or replaced since.
    • The 3D models are simplified drawings from each bridge's size and type. They show which part has the problem, not the exact spot.
    • Cameras point at traffic and may not show the bridge itself. They go offline from time to time.
    • One bridge has no map location, two may sit a few miles off, and one has a traffic count that looks wrong (600,500 a day). These are marked where they appear.
